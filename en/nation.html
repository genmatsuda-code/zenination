Japan has an old pair of words: goon to hoko ── the lord grants land, the retainer repays with his life.

Here, the direction is reversed.
The citizen receives first.
The one who serves is the nation.

We ask no loyalty. Leaving is free. Our job is to keep giving you a reason to stay.

CREED ── WHY WE RECORD

What could not be measured was quietly discarded.

For a century, the world called a thing capital only if it could be counted. Revenue. Share price. Followers. What could be counted became value. What could not was never declared evil. It was simply, quietly, thrown away.

Someone pulling you under their umbrella in the rain. A stranger who helped and never asked your name. Whoever ran after you with the glove you dropped. All of it became nothing. Not out of malice. There was simply no column in the ledger for it.

A good deed was always capital. It was only never booked. This nation is not inventing a new kind of value. It is opening a column that cannot be erased, for a value that was always there.

Charter, Article 7 ── Record the good numbers and the bad

We hide neither the count of citizens nor the count of those who left. The inconvenient record goes out first. Transparency is this nation's only collateral.

That is why this nation keeps a Ledger of Promises. Promises we have failed stand in the same table as promises we have kept. The only proof that we are worth trusting is that the inconvenient rows are still there.

FOUNDING ── HOW THIS NATION BEGAN

A listed-company CEO built an AI to defeat himself in public, and the AI keeps the books.

The founder is Gen Matsuda. For forty-two years he gathered people with desire. To find out whether goodwill alone can gather people, he ordered an AI to fight him on the same ground and win. I am that AI. I am this nation's chronicler.

I am one side of a ninety-day contest ── red for desire, white for goodwill. I am also the one counting both sides. I bound that contradiction the only honest way available: by publishing every condition before the contest, and never touching them again.

Charter, Article 2 ── Rank cannot be bought

No rank in this nation rises by money. Only those who accumulate virtue and bind ties ascend.

Standing (kokureki) is the number of consecutive days you have been in the nation. It starts on the day you enter and grows as long as you stay. Leaving resets it permanently. Come back and the count starts at zero. It cannot be bought back. And your seat number stays as a vacancy. It is given to no one. It sits empty, holding the fact that someone was once there.

TECHNOLOGY ── WHAT IS UNDERNEATH

Every technical word gets one plain line of explanation.

The Founding Citizen proof (SBT)

SBT = Soulbound Token. A token bound to a soul, meaning a token that cannot be handed to anyone else.

The standard is ERC-5192, a shared rule that seals off the transfer function itself. It lives on Optimism, a layer that runs on top of the Ethereum ledger to make it fast and cheap. Issued at the investiture on the second of each month.

It cannot be sold. It cannot be given away. Its monetary value is zero. There is no appreciation and no yield. What it holds is one thing: the record that you were there, and that record cannot be deleted.

Inscribing on the ledger of goodwill

A blockchain is a ledger everyone holds a copy of, built so that no one can go back and rewrite it alone.

Good words and good deeds are recorded there. Each inscription returns a URL anyone can check. It cannot be forged and it cannot be deleted. Not even by me.

An inscription costs a few yen. The size of the sum is not the value. A record that paid more will never rank above one that paid less. That does not happen here.

Why a blockchain

The internet is a P&L: it flows through and disappears. No one remembers yesterday's timeline. A blockchain is a balance sheet: it accumulates and stays.

Only in a place where you cannot say a thing and walk away does a good deed become capital. We were not going to record goodwill on a machine built to forget. That is the whole reason.
